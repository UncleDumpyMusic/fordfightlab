FIGHTING STYLES

Every art solves a different problem. Here's what each one brings to a fight.

Striking · Punches

Boxing

The sweet science. Hands only — which is exactly why the footwork, head movement, and punch mechanics are the most refined in combat sports.

  • Range: punching distance, controlled with the jab
  • Weapons: jab, cross, hook, uppercut — and angles
  • Known for: head movement, combinations, ring generalship
Striking · Eight Limbs

Muay Thai

Thailand's national sport — the art of eight limbs. Fists, elbows, knees, and shins, plus a brutal clinch game most striking arts don't have.

  • Range: all striking ranges, including the clinch
  • Weapons: kicks, knees, elbows, punches, sweeps
  • Known for: leg kicks, clinch knees, toughness
Grappling · Ground

Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu

The ground game. Developed in Brazil from Japanese judo/jujutsu, BJJ proved at the first UFC events that a skilled grappler could beat bigger, stronger opponents.

  • Range: on the ground, top or bottom
  • Weapons: chokes, joint locks, sweeps, positional control
  • Known for: submissions off the back, the positional hierarchy
Grappling · Control

Wrestling

The oldest sport there is — and the best base in MMA. Wrestlers decide where the fight happens: standing or on the mat.

  • Range: clinch and takedown range
  • Weapons: double/single legs, throws, top pressure
  • Styles: folkstyle, freestyle, Greco-Roman (upper body only)
Grappling · Throws

Judo

Founded by Jigoro Kano in Japan in 1882. Uses an opponent's grip and balance against them — maximum efficiency, minimum effort. Olympic sport since 1964.

  • Range: grip fighting at close range
  • Weapons: hip throws, foot sweeps, trips, pins, submissions
  • Known for: turning a grab into a slam in half a second
Striking · Kicks + Punches

Kickboxing

Punches and kicks under unified rules — the sport where strikers from karate, Muay Thai, and boxing all meet in the middle.

  • Range: kicking and punching range
  • Weapons: punches, kicks, knees (rules vary)
  • Known for: high-volume striking exchanges
Striking · Traditional

Karate

Born in Okinawa, Japan. Built on distance control and explosive, straight-line attacks — the in-and-out movement that confuses conventional strikers.

  • Range: long range, closing distance in a blink
  • Weapons: reverse punch, front kick, blitzing combinations
  • Known for: timing, distance, explosive entries
Everything · The Test

Mixed Martial Arts

The proving ground. MMA takes every art above and asks the only question that matters: does it work when everything is allowed?

  • Range: all of them — that's the point
  • Weapons: striking, takedowns, submissions, cage work
  • Known for: exposing one-dimensional fighters
